A repetition is material to work with. Keep several clips connected to one pattern, or make a copy independent when it needs to change. Move between shaping a rule and choosing a single note.

01

Begin with a small rule

Spread five hits across sixteen steps. Walk through a scale. Connect a chord progression. Start with a seed you can hear.

02

Turn it into a variation

Rotate, reverse, transpose or stretch the pattern. Changes reach every clip that shares it; independent copies can take their own direction.

03

Take the notes into your hands

Keep a phrase you like as editable notes. Move a note, change its length or velocity, then place your patterns along the timeline and finish a piece.

02 / ROOM BETWEEN THE NOTES

Try nineteen steps
to the octave.

There are places to put a note between the familiar twelve. Choose an equal division, a scale and a reference frequency. Edit fractional pitches and carry them into playback and export without rounding them to twelve notes.

Glass Tides uses seven scale steps from nineteen equal divisions. Hear the idea as a complete piece below.

1 step = 1200 / 19 ≈ 63.16 centsOne step in 19-EDO is about 63.16 cents; a semitone in 12-EDO is 100 cents.

Does it run entirely in a browser?

The browser displays the editor. A native Haskell engine on the same PC computes the sound and sends it to the audio device. Start both using Launch.cmd in the download.

How do I save and export?

Save an .aya project: a JSON file that includes imported WAV assets. Export 48 kHz stereo WAVs and individual track stems. MIDI file import and export are also available.

Can I use plug-ins or external recording devices?

This first release focuses on built-in sounds, imported WAVs and PC-keyboard playing/recording. VST, ASIO, microphone recording and automatic external MIDI-device connection are not available. Microtonal MIDI exchange requires compatible pitch-bend handling at the receiving end.

Can I compose offline?

Yes. Once downloaded and extracted, the app needs neither an account nor an internet connection. Your project and imported audio stay in your local project file.

Where are the source and licenses?

Read the GitHub repository. Aya’s own code is MIT-licensed; bundled dependencies retain their own licenses. Copyright, license and redistribution information is included in the ZIP, the third-party guide and the licenses directory.
